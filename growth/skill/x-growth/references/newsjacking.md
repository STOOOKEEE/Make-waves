# Reacting to market news

Trading/crypto news is a standing content source: attention already exists, we just need an angle.
Series definition is `growth/context/04-content-engine.md` §7. This file is the craft.

---

## 1. The window

Attention on a story decays fast — most of it inside the first few hours, and X's ranker heavily
favours recent, in-conversation content.

- **Best:** while people are still forming an opinion. Our take helps shape the conversation.
- **Fine:** the same day, if the take is genuinely good.
- **Dead:** next morning. A great take 18 hours late reads as an account that found out late.

**Corollary: speed beats polish here.** A rough post inside the window outperforms a perfect one
outside it. This is the one content type where "ship it" wins over "improve it."

## 2. Only react when we have an angle nobody else has

This is the whole filter, and it kills most stories. Generic commentary is worse than silence — it's
the exact "off-topic candidate" behaviour the ranker hard-drops, and it makes the account
indistinguishable from every other crypto reply-guy.

**Our angle is almost always the same one, and it's genuinely unusual:**
we're the people watching this from inside a game where none of it is real. That gives us three
positions nobody else can take honestly:

1. **The absurdist.** We can find the market funny because we have nothing at stake in it.
2. **The translator.** A confusing story explained badly-but-correctly (see Explain It Badly). The
   news gives us a reason to teach without it feeling like a lesson.
3. **The mirror.** "our paper traders did exactly this last week and it went about as well."

**If none of the three fit, skip the story.** Skipping is free.

## 3. Take or explainer — pick one

**Take** — short, opinionated, funny. Works when the story is already understood and the value is the
angle.
```
everyone's calling it a crash. it's a tuesday. this is what a tuesday
looks like when you have leverage on
```

**Explainer** — the story as a teaching moment. Works when the story is confusing and half the
timeline is pretending to understand it. Higher save rate, better for the locked-out audience.
```
"funding rates went negative" means everyone betting it goes up is now
paying everyone betting it goes down, for the privilege of being wrong
together
```

Don't hybrid them. A take with an explanation stapled on is neither.

## 4. The hard rule: never dunk on real losses

When the story is people losing real money — liquidation cascade, exchange blowing up, a rug, someone
publicly wiped out — **we do not make jokes at their expense.**

We are the account where losing is free and funny. Turning that on people for whom it wasn't free is
the single fastest way to become the villain, and it torches the exact goodwill the whole voice is
built on.

- ✅ Dunk on **the industry**, on **ourselves**, on **bots**, on **the absurdity of the system**.
- ❌ Never on the person who just got liquidated.

If a story is mostly human damage, either post something warm or post nothing. Nothing is usually
right.

## 5. Also don't

- **Don't hashtag-ride.** Using trending tags to farm attention is explicitly named as manipulation
  (`guardrails.md` §1).
- **Don't newsjack unrelated stories.** Off-topic posts get hard-dropped by the ranker `[CODE]`, and
  the account's topic coherence is an asset (`accounts.md` §3).
- **Don't predict.** "this is going to keep dumping" is a call. We don't make calls — `growth/context/05-facts.md`.
- **Don't react to every story.** Two or three a week that land beat daily commentary nobody asked
  for. Silence is a position.
- **Don't pivot the take toward the product.** "…and this is why you should paper trade" undoes the
  whole post. Let the account's existence be the product placement.

## 6. The daily loop (~10 min)

1. Scan what markets/crypto twitter is actually arguing about right now.
2. For each candidate: **do we have one of the three angles?** If not, drop it.
3. Take or explainer. Write it fast.
4. Gate it (`growth/context/02-voice.md`), especially the villain test.
5. Ship, or drop it and move on. Don't hold a take overnight — it expires.
