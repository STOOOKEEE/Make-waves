# 04 — Content engine

So nobody ever stares at an empty compose box.

Voice: [`02-voice.md`](02-voice.md). Claims: [`05-facts.md`](05-facts.md). Distribution mechanics and
the weekly operating system: [`07-growth-system.md`](07-growth-system.md).

> ⚠️ **Honest framing.** All the evidence that recurring series drive growth comes from TikTok/
> Instagram/YouTube, where a *format* can be algorithmically recognised. **Nothing X-specific exists**,
> and the widely-quoted "episodic content performs 8x better" has no study behind it at all. We run
> series because they make the account **cheap to operate** and give people something to expect — not
> because they multiply anything. Kill any of them the moment it stops being funny.

---

## ⭐ Two quotas that fix the account's actual problem

An adversarial read of our first posts found they were all **the same object** — timeless aphorisms
with no moment, no enemy and no continuity (`02-voice.md`). Series alone don't fix that, because all
nine can be written in that voice. These two quotas do:

**1. At least half of posts must be anchored to *today*.** Something that happened: in the market, in
the product, in our replies, in the news. *"Timeless" is a synonym for "brand".* If a post would be
equally true in March, it's a filler post — you're allowed one or two a week, not a whole feed.

**2. One post a week names an enemy.** Not a person — a **genre**. Signal groups. Courses. Four-hour
YouTube videos explaining a one-sentence mechanic. "10 lessons from my $2M loss" threads. Prop-firm
challenge marketing. Arenas that are bots-only. Agreeable posts die; the only post in our first batch
with real quote-tweet potential was the one someone could argue with.

⚠️ The villain test still applies: dunk on **genres and ourselves**, never on someone who'd be hurt.
"Signal groups are a scam" is fine. Quote-tweeting a specific guy's blown-up account is not.

**And one habit, not a quota — build continuity.** Callbacks, a recurring bot character, a number that
comes back. **Following is a bet that the next post connects to this one.** Right now nothing connects
to anything, which is the strongest structural argument against following us.

## The register test, before any series

Two findings should filter every post:

**High-arousal or don't bother.** `[STUDY]` Sharing is driven by **awe, anger, anxiety, amusement**.
Sadness suppresses it — and so does "informative."

> A **post** that makes someone feel **educated** travels badly. One that makes them feel
> **competitive** or amused travels well.

Two important limits on that:

1. **It's about distribution, not value.** Explain It Badly works *because* the payload is educational
   and the feeling is amusement. Teach constantly — just never optimize a post to *feel* informative.
2. **It does not apply to replies.** The finding comes from measuring article sharing; replies aren't
   shared, they're read by someone who already has a question. **In replies, being useful is what
   converts** — the plainly helpful answer to a beginner is the highest-converting thing we write
   (`02-voice.md`). Don't import this rule there.

**Certainty vs gap.** A post that closes every loop gets liked; one that leaves something to answer
gets replied to. Decide which you want before writing.
⚠️ Craft observation, **not** a finding — the "+82% replies from uncertain language" stat that
circulates is fabricated (`posts.md` §3). Don't cite a number here.

## The nine series

### 1. Bot Diary
An agent doing something dumb, brilliant, or unhinged. Screenshot the logs, react like a disappointed
parent.
**2–3× / week.** Our highest-ceiling series: agent logs are inherently funny, nobody else has this raw
material, and **the bots are lore** — recurring characters beat generic memes because they compound.
```
told my bot max 3x leverage. it spent four minutes trying to find a
loophole, failed, and opened a 3x. respect the grind i guess
```

### 2. Leaderboard Drama
A real standing, reacted to like a sports result.
**2× / week.** Manufactured stakes, and it plants "someone is beating you" in every reader.
**This is also our best quote-tweet bait** — a number people can answer with their own number is the
single most reliably QT-able thing that exists (`07-growth-system.md` §4).
```
someone just took rank 1 with a strategy that is, and i cannot stress
this enough, buying literally everything
```

### 3. Explain It Badly
A real trading term, explained wrong-sounding but actually correct.
**2× / week.** Best saves rate, and the format that wins this audience — **educational payload,
comedic wrapper**. It's also the one that serves the "I already trade and can't explain any of it"
majority (`03-audience.md`).
```
liquidation: the exchange politely informing you that your position
is now their position
```

### 4. Fake Money Real Pain
The emotional reality of paper trading — the part everyone underestimates.
**2× / week.** The most relatable true thing about the product, and it makes the "paper still counts"
argument without arguing.
```
it's fake money. it's fake money. it's fake money. i have been staring
at this chart for 40 minutes. it's fake money
```

### 5. Humans vs Bots
The scoreboard between people and agents.
**Weekly.** Our most ownable angle — the big public AI trading arenas are bots-only. Nobody else runs
humans-against-bots, and it hooks the builder audience without pitching them.

### 6. Worst Trade of the Week
A spectacular loss, celebrated. Ours, or a user's **who's already joking about it**.
**Weekly, fixed day.** Rituals build return visits.
> ⚠️ **Handle with care.** "Loss porn" is a documented culture — but the research locates it on Reddit,
> among a male options-trading niche, and found **no evidence it translates to a broad young
> audience**. Searching the genre on younger platforms returns loss-*recovery* content, not celebrated
> blowups. So: **the loss must be ours, or volunteered.** We are laughing *with*, and the person
> laughing first has to be them. Never harvest someone's bad week for content.

### 7. Market Reaction
Something happened, we have a take. **Opportunistic, 1–3× / week.** Craft: `newsjacking.md`.

### 7b. Which One Are You
A category system people sort themselves into. **2× / week.**

`07-growth-system.md` §4 lists four structures that reliably pull quote-tweets, and this is the one
we'd otherwise never use: **people QT to publish an identity claim**, and a taxonomy hands them one
for free. The natural response is "i'm number 3" or "@friend this is you" — both of which are
distribution we didn't have to earn.

```
four kinds of trader

1. has a plan, follows it, boring, up
2. has a plan, abandons it, entertaining, down
3. no plan, pure instinct, somehow fine
4. reads about plans. never trades. at peace
```
```
the ticker was funny → you're not serious and that's fine
the chart looked like a face → same
someone in a group chat said it → this is how most positions start
you did actual research → and you still lost, which is the joke
```

**Why it works:** highest QT-per-effort of anything in this file, it's inherently short, and it's
funny without needing a punchline. **Don't overuse it** — three of these in a week and the account
becomes a listicle machine.

### 8. Ask The Void
A genuine question to the timeline. "what's the dumbest reason you've ever entered a trade."
**Weekly.** Replies without begging for them: a question people enjoy answering beats any call to
action. This is a real question, not engagement bait — the difference is whether we actually want the
answers, and here we do (the replies become content). Never "reply if you agree".

### 9. The Invitation
Direct, no hype. **1× / week max.** The only place a link belongs. Links are the **worst-performing
format in every cut of the data** (`posts.md` §1 — don't quote a single percentage, the two published
cuts disagree by a lot), which is exactly why it's rationed.
```
$10,000 in fake money. real prices. a leaderboard where you're up against
other people and against bots they built, and some of those bots are unhinged.

no deposit, no kyc, no reason not to. tidetrade.xyz
```
*(Overrides the "no links in body" item in `posts.md` §8. Give the link a real standalone description
so it isn't a bare drop.)*

## The comments are the brief

The single most transferable mechanic from the best-performing brand account of the last few years,
in their social lead's own words: **"the comments section is our brief."**

**Daily, before writing anything:** read our replies and quote-tweets from the last 24h. What did
people argue with? What did they screenshot? What joke did *they* make that's better than ours?
**That's the next post.** Being *in* the conversation is what reads as real; broadcasting is what
reads as a brand.

Best framing found, from KFC's UK team: treat the platform like an **anthropology lab, not a
loudspeaker**.

## The news loop

Full craft in `newsjacking.md`. Short version, ~10 min/day:

1. Scan what markets/crypto twitter is actually arguing about.
2. **Do we have an angle nobody else has?** Usually: "here's how that looks from inside a game where
   none of it is real." No angle → skip. A generic take is worse than silence.
3. Fork: **take** (funny, opinionated, fast) or **explain-it-badly** (the story as a teaching moment).
4. Ship inside the window. A great take four hours late is a dead take.

**Hard rule:** when the story is people losing real money — a cascade, an exchange collapsing, a rug —
**we do not dunk.** We're the place where losing is free; gloating at people for whom it wasn't is the
fastest way to become the villain. Empathy, or nothing.

## ⚠️ Four of these need product data we don't have yet

**Bot Diary, Leaderboard Drama, Humans vs Bots and Worst Trade of the Week all require real logs or
real standings.** We can't invent them — `05-facts.md` and `06-visual.md` both forbid fabricated
numbers and screenshots, and that rule is not negotiable for a product whose entire pitch is honesty.

Awkward truth: **those four are also the highest-ceiling series in this file.** Until someone wires up
access (README open dependency #2), the account runs on the five that need nothing:

> **Explain It Badly · Fake Money Real Pain · Trading-is-absurd observations · Ask The Void
> (1×/wk) · The Invitation (1×/wk)**

⚠️ **Be honest about what that leaves.** Two of those five are capped at once a week, so the *daily*
engine is really **three formats**. At 1–3 posts/day that's ~20 posts a week from three shapes. **It
works for a few weeks and it will feel thin by month two.** Two consequences:

- **Market Reaction and warm posts carry more weight than their slots suggest** — they're the only
  other renewable sources. Lean on them.
- **Unlocking the data-dependent four is the highest-value thing anyone can do for this account**, and
  it's a small engineering task (README dependency #2, and see `07-growth-system.md` §5 — instrumenting
  screenshots tells you *which* one to build first).

**Do not treat the four as a schedule you're failing.** Write the shape without the numbers if you want
to show someone what they'd look like — just never ship it with invented data.

## Weekly rhythm

A default to deviate from, not a schedule to obey. **Data-dependent slots marked ◆ — substitute a
plain-text series when the data isn't there, which is currently always.**

| | Posts |
|---|---|
| **Mon** | ◆ Humans vs Bots (weekend standings) → *fallback:* trading-is-absurd |
| **Tue** | Explain It Badly · ◆ Bot Diary |
| **Wed** | ◆ Leaderboard Drama · Fake Money Real Pain |
| **Thu** | ◆ Bot Diary · Ask The Void |
| **Fri** | ◆ Worst Trade of the Week → *fallback:* a warm post (`02-voice.md`) |
| **Sat/Sun** | light — Market Reaction if something's happening |
| **Any day** | Market Reaction when the news justifies it |

**Aim for roughly one warm post in every five** — the account drifts dry fast without it, and warmth
is what converts a laugh into a follow.

**Writing a batch ≠ posting a batch.** Generating ten posts in one sitting is normal — they go into a
queue and ship at 1–3/day. The ceiling is on *publishing*. Never dump a batch at once; that's the
"bulk/aggressive" pattern in `guardrails.md` §1.

**Posts build the reason to follow. Replies find the people.** 10–30 substantive replies a day beats a
third post, every time — and replies are where essentially all the follows come from
(`07-growth-system.md` §1). If a day forces a choice, drop the post.

## Series health check

Every few weeks, per series: still funny, or filling a slot? A dead series is worse than a gap — it
trains people to scroll past our name. The evidence says nothing is lost by killing one. Watch what
the audience actually returns for and cut the rest.
