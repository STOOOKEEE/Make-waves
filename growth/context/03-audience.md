# 03 — Audience

Who we want, what they actually feel, and what makes them stop scrolling.

## Three assumptions that were wrong

Worth stating plainly, because the obvious version of this positioning is off.

### ❌ "They need to be coaxed into trading"
**They already trade — more than anyone.** `[A]` **47% of Gen Z investors trade at least weekly, 14%
daily** (vs boomers 15% / 5%). 82% of US Gen Z investors started before age 21. 51% own or have owned
crypto — the highest of any generation.

> **The reframe: not "learn to start." It's "you're already doing this — do it here first, where
> losing is free."** Positioning as beginner education targets a smaller, less engaged segment than
> the one that's already trading, badly, with real money.

And yet `[A]` **73% of Gen Z investors still say they don't know where or how to begin** — *while
holding investments*. Both things are true at once. They're doing it and they feel like they're
faking it. That gap is our whole opening.

### ❌ "Jargon is the barrier"
Jargon is real but ranks **third**. The actual ranking `[A]/[B]`:
1. **Fear of losing money / making a bad call — 37%**
2. **No spare money to invest — 29%**
3. Intimidation by the market — 29% (highest of any generation)
4. Distrust of institutions — ~20%

> **Lead with stakes removed, not complexity removed.** "You can't lose anything" attacks barriers 1
> and 2 directly. "We explain the words" attacks the third-place one. We have been leading with the
> wrong barrier.

### ❌ "They hate the financial system, so lean anti-establishment"
`[B]` **62% of Gen Z trust financial advisors — more than any other generation.** Institutional
distrust is a minority position. The dominant emotion isn't rage, it's **feeling behind**.

Mock the *absurdity* of trading culture. Don't run a burn-it-down account — it's a smaller audience
than it looks and it overlaps with exactly the segment regulators are circling.

## What they actually feel

This is the emotional centre of gravity, and it should shape everything:

- `[B]` **43% report "money dysmorphia"** — a distorted sense of their own finances — vs 25% Gen X.
  95% of those affected say it damages their real financial behaviour.
- `[B]` **80% of Gen Z who pick risky investments say they feel "financially behind"** and believe
  speculative bets will get them there faster than stocks. One study names this **financial nihilism**.
- `[A]` **69% of aspiring investors report negative emotions** — frustration, anxiety, helplessness —
  just thinking about starting.
- `[A]` **Only 6% say they don't compare themselves to other investors.** Comparison is near-universal.

**So the feeling to design for is not fear and not greed. It's *behindness*.**

Which cuts two ways, and we have to hold both:
- It's why a leaderboard works — comparison is already how they think.
- It's why we must never sell catching up. "Get ahead" framing feeds the nihilism the research
  documents, and that's the same emotional engine regulators are worried about.

**Our version:** the leaderboard is a *game*, explicitly, and everyone's playing with fake money.
Comparison without consequence. That's the honest offer and it's genuinely better than the alternative.

## Who we're talking to

### 1. Already-trading, quietly lost (primary)
Young, online, has a Robinhood or crypto app, trades more often than they'd admit, and would not be
able to explain half of what they're doing. Feels behind. Compares constantly.

**Stops their scroll:** something that names the feeling exactly. A term explained in a way that makes
them go "oh that's it?". Someone losing spectacularly. A leaderboard number they think they could beat.

**Repels them:** being condescended to. Beginner framing. Anything that implies they don't already
trade. Anything that sounds like a course.

**Needs to hear:** it's free, nothing's real, and you can find out whether you're actually any good
without paying for the answer.

### 2. Curious but never started
Wants in, hasn't, because of barriers 1 and 2 — no money and fear of losing it.

**Stops their scroll:** the fact that it costs nothing and risks nothing. Humor that makes trading
look approachable instead of intimidating.

**Repels them:** charts with drawings, jargon assumed, anything smelling of money-making.

### 3. Competitive crypto/trading natives (secondary)
Already here, deeply competitive, allergic to being sold to. They'll adopt Tide as a **status game**.

**Stops their scroll:** leaderboard drama, a number to beat, a good dunk on the industry, bot standings.

**Repels them:** being taught, "beginner-friendly" framing, brand-account cadence.

### 4. AI-agent builders (tertiary, high-leverage)
Small group, disproportionate reach, build in public.

**Stops their scroll:** an agent doing something genuinely interesting or genuinely stupid. Logs. A bot
beating humans, or getting beaten by them.

**Repels them:** "MCP-native" as a headline. They know what MCP is.

> **Write for #1.** #2 comes along for the tone, #3 for the competition, #4 for the bots.

## Two things that change how we acquire

**Influencers underperform peer proof.** `[B]` Gen Z trusts **customer reviews 72%** vs **influencer
content 55%**, and only 23% think celebrity promotion is genuine. Finance is the category where
influencer trust is most damaged. **Visible real users beat paid creators** — which is convenient,
because our product generates visible real users by default (see `07-growth-system.md` §5).

**Our real competitive set is prop firms, not Investopedia.** `[C]` The fastest-growing youth-facing
segment in trading is prop-firm challenges — free-entry competition as top of funnel, marketed through
Discord and trader-founder authenticity, at scale with a young audience. Their "funded" accounts are
usually still simulated. **We are not first, and pretending otherwise is a claim we'd lose.** Study
free-competition-as-acquisition as a proven format rather than inventing one.

## Where they are

`[A]` Where Gen Z investors actually learn: **YouTube 67% · TikTok 47% · Reddit 29%** — financial
advisors only 24%.

X is not where they learn; it's where the competitive, already-trading slice argues. That's fine — it's
audience #1 and #3 — but it means **X is a conversation channel, not an education channel.** Don't
write X posts as lessons.

Venues worth targeting (verify current handles before building a list — these move):
- Finance-adjacent **meme** accounts — the audience for "trading is absurd" is far larger than the
  audience for trading
- Competitive trading / leaderboard culture
- AI-agent and builder circles
- Prop-firm and trading-challenge communities

## What we're not chasing

- **Pure reach.** A viral post outside the niche converts terribly (documented: 4% → 0.6%).
- **Existing profitable traders.** They don't need paper. Welcome for the jokes, not the target.
- **The "make money online" crowd.** Wrong promise, and courting them makes us look like the thing
  we're positioned against.
- **The financial-nihilism segment, on its own terms.** They're a real and large group, but selling
  "catch up fast" to people who feel behind is how this category earns its worst headlines.
